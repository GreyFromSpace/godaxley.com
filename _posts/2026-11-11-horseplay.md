## Born

I was born March 26th, 1996, at 7:33 PM, to Jerry and Holly. Holly was 17. Her parents were Nancy and Bob Woods. They divorced, and Bob later killed himself. No idea why. Holly was raised by Brett Denhalter.

Very soon after I was born, I was kidnapped by my grandparents. No warning, no explanation. They just showed up. I had been left alone with some random tweaker while the other two did god knows what. They raised me from then on. Brett and Nancy were mom and dad to me. I'd known before that, but around 11 years old it really clicked. Sitting on the playground, spacing out, when it finally fell into place.

For the most part they were pretty normal boomers. You'd probably guess the type. The fat guy with male pattern baldness, and the pretty wife who always wears makeup and puts her hair in curlers every day. Almost a proto-Stepford wife, except instead of the 1950s, we've evolved into the 1980s housewife, which is what she was while she raised Holly. Minus the cigarettes. She has a job, she isn't expected to stay home all day, but she's still expected to do all the wifely, womanly things. So she cooked. She cleaned. She raised the children. Brett went and taught school all day, then came home and got upset about things.

My earliest memories of Brett are of a loud, scary man who came home every day. His mood decided how the rest of the night would go. If he was upset, we heard arguing. We heard him complain. He was never shy about it when things didn't please him.

He ran his house like a business. Not quite a prison, but close. He never said it out loud, but the message was always there: everyone is an employee here, and we're all a family, but we have rules. You can't make us look bad. You follow our religion. If you don't believe in it, you shut up and pretend. What he actually said was how important it is to remember who you are, and that you just do things for family without question, because that's what family does.

I really was an employee. My brother Samuel is nonverbal autistic, and the entire time, I was made to help with him. Anytime I was asked, no matter when, why, or how. On call, 24/7 respite care. For free, most of my life.

We all were employees, all four of us. Noah, Josh, Brittany, and me. All of us were unpaid interns on the Denhalter ranch. We spent our days at school, then got used for chores, then got rewarded with TV or video games. If we stayed up too late, which little boys sometimes do, we were bent over the knee and spanked into submission. That was our entire growing up. It didn't stop us, either. We just got better and better and better at hiding it.

Also I need to clarify that when I say "chores" I'm not talking about like, doing the dishes or putting your laundry away. That's all normal shit. Though when it came to anything that required a device that could ever possibly wear out or break they'd freak out. It was more important that they had full control over whether that appliance degraded or not. Every use was scrutinized and so we never were taught how to use a washing machine, or how to operate a lawn mower. They didn't teach me to drive either, probably for the same fucking reason. The cars were a huge tension point too. Felt like they were more important than us. We didn't ever get trusted with any of that. No machines got used by us kids. We were never really taught how to care for ourselves. That was stuff the world was supposed to teach us. We were taught how to keep the house from falling apart while the adults handled the money making and the "dealing with everything" and they kept the whole work and financial thing basically out of our purview. That wasn't our business. We never knew if we were rich or doing well or what. We just knew they handled those things. And now they wonder why I'm unable to care for myself properly... and I'm 30... turns out that shaming your kids for not just being correct isn't how you fucking teach them anything.

The chores we were made to do, ON TOP OF CARING FOR SAMUEL. A task that was actually dangerous sometimes. We would have to do hard physical labor. Clearing swaths of blackberry brambles with gloves, by hand. The gloves were NOT thick enough by the way so we got injured a lot. It was tough. This was a punishment used when we were out of things to be grounded from. I've spent entire MONTHS grounded and honestly never remembered quite why...All I remember is that nothing is mine, everything is leverage and my life isn't mine. My dignity is ephemeral and I learned that word early on in life.

## Seventeen

No. Actually, it took me until I was 27 to get my high school diploma, because at 17 I had a full-blown meltdown after being pushed to my breaking point, and I wanted out.

It happened on a very bad day. Brett needed help outside with the overgrown area between the fence and the side of the house. It covers two walls of the house, and it was filled with brambles, trees of heaven, random horrors and such. Not fun.

I couldn't get my shoes on, because the knot in the laces was tied into this teeny, tight ball that wasn't any kind of bow, so it was impossible to loosen. I worked at it for like fifteen minutes. Brett came in all hot and angry, accusing me of defiance. I tried to explain, but I was choking up, because I was trying to get done faster, and his insisting made my hands shake, which made it even more impossible. I got overwhelmed and threw the shoe on the ground.

He got fucking upset. I was upset. I don't know how it calmed down, but he was made to understand that the situation wasn't my fault. I was still in trouble.

I got my shoes fixed, but I was really, really upset and overwhelmed. The work was hard, and Noah kept making the whole thing into an argument, which made us both angry at each other. So I got a stick and hit a tree stump, because I was trapped in an abusive situation and needed release, or escape.

Brett treated that as an act of aggression, a threat. He started treating me like an active shooter. I screamed and freaked out, and they just fucking blamed me for all of it.
